# The Assurance Model Was Built for a World That No Longer Exists

A research narrative on the origins, expansion, and declining epistemic reach of periodic audit — and what AI does to the mismatch.

Read it: **https://rmhrisk.github.io/assurance-model/**

## What this is

For fifty years the scale, speed, reach, complexity, and consequence of systems grew faster than the methods used to assure them. Institutions responded by adding controls, audits, certifications, reports, and regulators. The obligations expanded. The method by which anything is actually known did not.

The post runs in four parts:

1. **The Bounded World** — what an audit actually promises, and the containment that made periodic examination plausible
2. **Everything Outgrew It** — six decades of acceleration, attach rate versus adoption, and assurance debt
3. **The Epistemic Problem** — the narrow claim and the broad signal, sampling, incomplete self-knowledge, and five cases that failed through five different mechanisms
4. **The Deficit and What Follows** — the four curves, accountability over adaptation, and AI as accelerant before it is anything else

A companion piece, *Why Continuous Assurance Did Not Happen Until Now*, covers the economics of cognition and what AI actually changes.
